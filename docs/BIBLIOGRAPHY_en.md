# Bibliography and conceptual provenance

> [← Back to the README](../README.md) · [Versión en español](./BIBLIOGRAPHY_es.md) · [LUS](./LUS_en.md) · [Affective genealogy](./GENEALOGY_en.md) · [Observer provenance](./OBSERVER_en.md)

These authors are interlocutors, **not borrowed authority**. LUS uses them to expose convergences, differences and tensions in its claims. A useful vocabulary does not prove the program, and no citation substitutes for evidence.

## The entry rule

Since 2026-08-22, **a work enters this registry by being arbitrated, not by being read**, and every entry writes down **where it loses** against the program's purpose. The deciding question is *what does this work force LUS to change?* — if the honest answer is “nothing, it confirms us”, it is decoration.

Why the rule is harsh on good sources: **a faithful summary of a major author is redundant literature with borrowed authority** — the summary already exists, better written, in the source. The only thing an arbitration produces that exists in neither body is **the disagreement**.

What cannot be arbitrated stays as **bibliography with its obstacle written down**, and that is a result of the program, not a debt.

## Foundational interlocutors

Cited in the corpus since its first formulation. **They are not arbitrated**: they were read *with* the program rather than against it, and that difference matters to anyone who wants to lean on them.

- **Martin Buber** · *I and Thou* (1923) — the **Between** supplies a relational starting point. LUS adopts the structure, not the theology, and gives the term a stricter operational boundary.
- **Louis Althusser** · “Ideology and Ideological State Apparatuses” (1970) — **interpellation** helps ask how an AI response places the human in a position of judgment and responsibility.
- **Gilbert Simondon** · *Individuation in Light of Notions of Form and Information* (1958) — **transduction** describes a structure advancing through successive transformations.
- **Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver** · *The Mathematical Theory of Communication* (1949) — signal, entropy and noise provide an analogy for filtering records. Their mathematical theory does **not** establish semantic value, model LLM context or prove Lore's architecture.
- **Gregory Bateson** · *Steps to an Ecology of Mind* (1972) — a difference that changes something downstream supplies a strong test for information. LUS imports the distinction; Bateson does not validate LUS as a whole.
- **Norbert Wiener** · *Cybernetics* (1948) — feedback makes error from real work part of the next state of a system.
- **Edgar Morin** · *Introduction to Complex Thought* (1990) and *Seven Complex Lessons in Education for the Future* — dialogic and organizational recursion widen the account of parts and wholes; the latter also informs the program's ethical orientation.
- **Andy Clark and David Chalmers** · “The Extended Mind” (1998) — an external store may participate in cognition when it is reliably coupled to action rather than merely available.
- **Hubert Dreyfus** · *What Computers Still Can't Do* (1992) — situated and tacit knowledge sharpens the boundary between general model capability and criteria acquired in practice.

## Arbitrated — enter as criteria, one claim each

Each contributes **a single** claim to LUS's `principios.md`, and each has its defeat written down.

- **Albert Camus** · *The Myth of Sisyphus* (1942), *The Misunderstanding* (1944), *Summer* — **`principios.md` #9**: *a system of criteria does not reduce the absurd; it knows what to do when the absurd appears*. **Where it loses:** Camus's *mesure* is an ethical-political limit on rebellion, not an epistemic routing rule. Adopted as a declared analogy, and it grounds no hypothesis.
- **Edwin Hutchins** · *Cognition in the Wild* (1995) — **#10, constitutive medium**: the properties of the medium determine what a functional system can compute, so **the loading mechanism is the object of the theory, not its noise**. Criteria that never loads is not a correct rule with bad luck. **Where it loses:** he is deliberately anti-normative — he describes how a system computes, never what *ought* to constrain a decision — and his unit of analysis dissolves the criteria/memory distinction the program rests on.
- **Karl Weick** · *Sensemaking in Organizations* (1995), “Mann Gulch” (1993), “Drop Your Tools” (1996) — **#11, the cost of letting go**: a body of criteria **makes reframing more expensive, and that cost peaks exactly when reframing is what saves you**. It names a hole in the program itself: its review instruments all assume an unhurried pass. **Where it loses:** his sensemaking is retrospective and hostile to anticipatory codification, and Lore is prospective by construction. The warning about the cost of letting go is adopted; the thesis that codifying is the error is not. And the analogy transfers a mechanism, not a magnitude: a team deciding about a document is not in a forest fire.
- **Heinz von Foerster** · *Observing Systems* (1981), “On Constructing a Reality” (1973), “Ethics and Second-Order Cybernetics” (1991) — **#12, bounded reflexivity**: situating the observer is **a control procedure, never an epistemology**. Using *“the observer participates”* as the explanation of a finding is forbidden: if it explained the findings, no finding could ever be wrong. **Where it loses:** he is a constructivist, and his ethical corollary replaces the question of truth. **He offers reflexivity and takes falsifiability with it** — the procedural half is adopted, the epistemological half rejected.

## Arbitrated — enter as bibliography, NOT as criteria

None of these authors governs a decision of the program. Every entry carries **the exact obstacle** that would have to be resolved for it to enter, and that open question is worth more than anything that could be adopted today.

- **Martin Heidegger** · *Being and Time* (1927) — finitude raised a useful question about the end of a Between. **Obstacle:** his death individualizes precisely because it is one's own and cannot be shared, and LUS studies a relation as its unit. **What would let it in:** how a relation can anticipate an ending that neither of its parts can anticipate on the other's behalf.
- **Daniel Wegner** · “Transactive Memory: A Contemporary Analysis of the Group Mind” (1987) — a group's memory depends partly on knowing who holds what. **Obstacle:** transactive memory **presupposes persistent, autonomous memory in each party**, and in a human–AI Between one party resets. It fails at its precondition, not at its conclusion. **What would let it in:** what a transactive directory is when one party does not persist.
- **Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela** · *Autopoiesis and Cognition* (1973/1980), *The Tree of Knowledge* (1984), *Principles of Biological Autonomy* (1979), *The Embodied Mind* (1991) — by some distance the vocabulary closest to the Between of anything the program has read. **Obstacle, and it is the hardest in the registry:** in enaction, knowledge **is not representation and therefore not transferable** — between structurally determined systems no information passes, only perturbation. **Lore is defined as transferable criteria**, so the collision happens exactly at the property their thesis depends on. A second obstacle: autopoiesis is defined for the living, and one of the two parties of the Between is not. **What would let them in:** how criteria can be transferable if only perturbation passes between structurally determined systems. If that is ever resolved on their terms, **the program stops saying that criteria travels** and reformulates two of its hypotheses.

> **Correction note — 2026-08-30.** Until this date this document presented Hutchins, Wegner, Weick and von Foerster as neutral “extended interlocutors”, and placed Varela among the foundational ones with a confirmatory reading — *“enaction resists treating criteria as inert content”*. **The arbitration found the opposite in two of those cases:** Weick warns that a Lore makes reframing more expensive, and enaction is the hardest objection the program has, not a support. The entries stand corrected and the date is left in place: a document coherent with itself can be false toward the outside and survive every rereading.

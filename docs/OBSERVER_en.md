# Observer provenance

> [← Back to the README](../README.md) · [Versión en español](./OBSERVER_es.md) · [LUS](./LUS_en.md) · [Bibliography](./BIBLIOGRAPHY_en.md) · [Affective genealogy](./GENEALOGY_en.md)

This is the third of the provenance registries LUS keeps separate, and the only one whose **content is not published**. What follows is the method; the registry itself lives in the program's repository and does not travel.

## Three different questions, three registries

Mixing them destroys the one thing each of them can do. The distinction is functional, not organizational.

| Registry | Question it answers | Entry rule |
|---|---|---|
| [Bibliography](./BIBLIOGRAPHY_en.md) | Who does the program argue with? | A work enters **by being arbitrated**, with where it loses written down |
| [Affective genealogy](./GENEALOGY_en.md) | What sensibility made certain questions thinkable? | A work enters if it **shaped** the sensibility and what changed can be named |
| **Observer provenance** *(this page)* | **What distinctions did the researcher already carry before observing the phenomenon?** | A commitment enters if it **predates** the program and can generate distinctions |

The bibliography can support a claim. The genealogy can say *“this visual decision came from here”* precisely because it never pretended to be a reason. This registry **does neither**: it supports nothing and explains no product decision.

## Why it exists

A program that studies a relation has a problem the others do not: **whoever observes the relation is part of the history that made it observable in that particular way.** Acknowledging this is not a confession, it is an instrument — it makes it possible to ask, case by case, whether a distinction in the corpus came from the phenomenon or from the repertoire the observer arrived with.

Its concrete use is **a single gate**: when the structure of a finding coincides with a commitment the researcher already held, the coincidence is written down next to the finding and **forces naming the rival explanation** that the coincidence makes more likely. It does not invalidate the observation; it demands a detector other than the one that produced it.

## What this registry does NOT authorize

- **It does not explain the phenomenon.** Situating the observer tells you **where** something was distinguished from, never why it is true.
- **It is not a theory of knowledge.** The program explicitly forbids using *“the observer participates”* as the explanation of a finding: if it explained them, no finding could ever be wrong and the hypothesis registry would lose its function. Reflexivity enters as a **bias-control procedure** and stops there.
- **It governs no decision.** It is marked `records`, not `governs`. That it never fires is not a defect of its own.
- **It does not turn a biography into an argument.** That something mattered before does not make it true afterwards.

## Why the method is published and the content is not

Because **its audience is not whoever reads this page: it is the program's own future sessions.** The registry exists to control the bias of the person investigating, not to inform a third party.

Publishing the **method** makes the discipline auditable: anyone can check that the gate exists, what it requires and what it forbids. Publishing the **content** would only expose a person without making anything more falsifiable — and a fact that does not change what can be refuted gains nothing by travelling.

When a claim of the program **does need** to state part of that repertoire in order to be falsifiable, the claim states it itself, at its minimum: the open hypothesis about the observer's prior repertoire declares the four commitments needed to look for them in another researcher's corpus — **a relational practice, a discipline of editing and condensation, two schools read years before the program** — and nothing else. That is the whole rule: **what travels is what its function requires, and not one datum more.**

> **The part worth keeping is that this coincidence was fortunate.** The cut that protects a person and the cut that makes a hypothesis falsifiable turned out to be the same one, so there was no choice to make between privacy and rigor. Nothing guarantees a repeat. If the falsifiable minimum of some future claim includes a datum that must not travel, the coincidence has broken and the person on record decides: narrow the claim to what can be stated, keep it unpublished and accept the gap, or authorize that specific datum. **What is not done is publishing a mutilated hypothesis that can no longer be false and still calling it a hypothesis.**

## If you keep your own Lore

None of this is a kit capability or a file Lore Plugin generates. It is a practice of the research program, written here in case the shape is useful.

What transfers is short: **a module's authority is declared, not inherited from the folder it lives in.** A module that only records — a moodboard, a reference list, a note about why you like something — marked as such stops being confused with criteria that governs, and a connectivity sweep stops counting it as orphaned. And if something must not leave your machine, **it lives in its own file that no manifest names**: a header saying “do not publish” is read by a person, and derived copies are not assembled by a person.
